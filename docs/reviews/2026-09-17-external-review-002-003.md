# External review of features 002 and 003 (2026-09-17)

Source: an outside reviewer (a Google fellow invited by the project owner) answering the brief
published for features 002 (investigation engine) and 003 (Datadog integration). Reproduced
verbatim below; the decisions it drove are recorded in the two specs' Clarifications sections
and in ADR-0003. Treat the text as the reviewer's opinion, adopted where the specs say so.

---

## Four things to settle before the seven questions

**Measure your ceiling first.** Take your last 20 real incidents and ask, for each, whether the true cause would have been a node in the graph at alert time. Flag flips, Terraform, DB migrations, cert expiries, cron, third-party outages and traffic shifts won't be there with only the OTel and Kubernetes feeders. That fraction is the hard upper bound on recall. If it's 50%, the next feeder is worth more than the reasoning layer. It's two hours of work, and it might reorder the roadmap.

**Rank against symptom onset, not alert time.** Monitors lag by minutes. Run change-point detection on the alerting metric first, then rank by distance to onset and exonerate anything after it. In the worked example, the storefront scaling change at 0.58 could well be an effect of the incident (the autoscaler reacting to retries). Autoscaler-originated changes should be typed differently from human-originated ones.

**The hypothesis space must be open.** Alongside the ranked candidates, carry an explicit "no observed change explains this" hypothesis with its own probability mass. Otherwise confidence is forced to sum to 1 over four candidates, and "unknown" becomes a prompt behaviour instead of a computed result.

**Trajectory replay is not world replay.** "Replayed against its recording with zero live calls" only holds if the agent asks the same queries. A non-deterministic investigator won't, so a second run hits queries that were never recorded. Two layers are needed:

- L1 records model outputs too, so replay is byte-identical and tests plumbing on every PR.
- L2 records a world: the full cross product of a small typed query algebra over the alert neighbourhood and time window — compare(pointer, window A, window B), new log patterns(pointer, window), error spans on an edge. Any reasoning path can then be served from it.
- A miss returns a typed `not_recorded`, distinct from `no_data`, and the miss rate becomes a fixture-quality metric.

This constrains what workers may ask, which is good for every other reason too.

## 1. Grading a non-deterministic investigator

Change the default. Majority-of-three is a noisy gate. A fixture the agent solves 80% of the time passes about 90% of the time. With eight such fixtures, CI goes green about 42% of the time with nothing regressed.

- Headline metric: pass@1, the mean over runs, because production gets one run.
- Reliability metric: pass^k (all k runs succeed).
- Best-of-k: never gate on it. It measures capability, not what the on-call experiences.
- Gate on the aggregate: 8 fixtures × 5 runs is 40 Bernoulli trials. That reliably detects a drop from 90% to 70% and cannot reliably detect 90% to 80%. Name the gate honestly for what it can detect.
- Extend only when needed: run 3 and go to 7 only when the first 3 disagree.
- Temperature: evaluate at the production temperature.

The metric. "Top 3" out of four candidates has a 75% random baseline. Worse, the deterministic ranker already puts the culprit first on every fixture, so on the current corpus the investigator can only tie or do harm.

- Lift over the prior: the investigator's MRR minus the ranker's MRR.
- Harm rate: the fraction of runs where the model talked itself out of a correct rank 1.
- Citation validity as a hard 100% gate: every claim resolves to an evidence id, and any number in the claim matches the digest.

Then build fixtures where the prior fails: a slow-burn change three hours earlier; a culprit three hops away; a decoy deploy two minutes before the alert on an adjacent service.

Ground truth a fixture should carry:

- Culprit: a change id, or `unobserved`, or `not_change_induced`, with a category.
- Causal path: the entity and edge chain from cause to symptom (grades localisation, attribution and mechanism separately, with partial credit).
- Decisive evidence: predicates over digests, not strings, plus the exonerating evidence for each decoy.
- Knowability time: the earliest observed-time instant at which the decisive evidence existed. If the decisive log pattern was ingested at 14:36, "unknown" at 14:34 is the correct answer.
- Provenance: synthetic, recorded or derived, plus the sanitiser policy version.

Multiply the corpus mechanically: delete the culprit event (expected answer becomes "unobserved change, symptoms localise to payments"); inject far-away decoys, shift all timestamps, permute entity names — none should change the verdict (name permutation tests structure vs guilty-sounding names).

Calibration: cannot be measured on eight incidents. Use Brier or log loss, paired across versions on the same fixtures. Don't let the model verbalise confidence: emit typed judgments (supports / refutes / neutral, with a strength, per hypothesis) and compute the posterior deterministically from prior and likelihood ratios. Ship coarse buckets until the corpus justifies two decimals.

## 2. Budgets for a P1

Numbers reasonable, but the shape should be anytime rather than deadline-driven. Steps 1–3 take 150 ms: publish the prior-only ranking within seconds, clearly labelled. Then fire a deterministic first wave in parallel with no model in the loop; for each top candidate: error rate and latency before/after; new log patterns; error spans on path edges; errors split by `version` tag (the closest thing to proof for a rollout hypothesis). The LLM's first turn starts with evidence in hand. Those queries are always in the fixture.

Two profiles: `page` (first tested hypothesis in 90–120 s, substantive answer by 5 min, hard stop at 10) and `review` (quality-bound, for postmortems and nightly evaluation).

What to cap: not a flat call count. Budget per backend and by cost class, cap query window width. The binding constraint is the vendor quota, not model cost (Datadog span search/aggregate: 300 req/h, shared with humans during the incident). Express the agent's budget as a share of the remaining quota read from response headers. One investigation per incident, not per alert. Operators cap wall time, cost, per-backend budget, concurrency, daily global spend — never the evidence requirement or read-only.

On exhaustion: keep a ~15% reserve for the closing synthesis; list untested hypotheses with the exact next queries as deep links; confidence widens rather than freezes. Record the stop reason as a typed event. Add a diminishing-returns stop. Distinguish query failed / no data / not yet ingested.

## 3. Human in the loop

Report-only; never a blocking "waiting for human" state. Add the inverse channel in v1: the on-call pushes facts at any time as typed, authenticated, non-blocking events in the evidence log; weight them as strong evidence, not truth; say so when telemetry contradicts them. Questions for the human go in the output as "what would resolve this"; an answer reopens the investigation. States: running, concluded (partial or final), reopened.

Rendering order: one-line verdict with the decisive fact and rollback candidate; ranked list with evidence for and against (exonerations prominent); a timeline of changes and symptom onset; narrative last. Every evidence item is a deep link to the exact query and window. Deliver in the incident thread, edit in place. One-click "was this right?" after resolution.

## 4. Orchestration shape

Most workers should not contain a model: graph (typed API), metrics (change-point + seasonality baseline = statistics), traces (algorithmic). Logs, docs, chat, postmortems: a model with its own context earns its place; mine templates algorithmically first. Reasoning stays in one agent with one belief state; parallelise evidence gathering, not reasoning. The worker boundary is simultaneously the replay boundary, the vendor abstraction, the sanitisation point and the prompt-injection barrier.

Where the hand-off loses information: pass the hypothesis and discriminating question in the call; make coverage mandatory in every digest (what was searched, data volume, sampling, truncation, lag); add drill-down handles and allow bounded sanitised exemplars on explicit request (a stack trace is often decisive); keep join keys (version tag, pod, trace ids, first-seen); allow one bounded free-text field flagged unverified.

The central data structure of 002 should be a hypothesis ledger held outside the context: hypotheses, priors, evidence ids for and against, status; updated through tool calls, re-rendered each turn; confidence computed from it; graders read it. Finish with a fresh-context verifier that checks each claim against its cited evidence.

## 5. Scope of the first recording campaign

Pick by incident density, not importance. Schedule fault-injection game days in staging. Record boring periods and false-positive alerts. Start the feeder now (topology history cannot be reconstructed later). Defer incidents as a graph source, but pull incident records as labels.

## 6. Sanitisation policy

People identifiers: dropped, not hashed (hashed email is still personal data). Infrastructure identifiers: keyed HMAC pseudonyms, consistent so joins survive. Log messages: mined templates with variables masked. Monitor bodies: dropped (keep name, query, thresholds, tags). Sanitise in the connector before disk; independent secrets/entropy scan at commit; canary tokens to test the sanitiser; the same boundary governs what goes to the model provider. Do not commit real production recordings to the public repo in v1: private corpus with private CI, publish synthetic structural twins. Owner sign-off as a signed manifest with policy version and hash; four-eyes when a second person exists.

## 7. Alert intake

Polling is fine for v1 (15–30 s poll is small next to monitor lag). Model intake as one more feeder emitting idempotent `alert.transition` events keyed on monitor, group and transition time; webhook and polling are two transports for the same event (push for speed, pull for truth). The webhook is a doorbell: it enqueues "poll now", the engine re-reads state from the API and never trusts the body; shared-secret header plus rate limiting suffices. Filter by tag; ignore flapping and no-data transitions; treat recoveries as evidence.

## Order of work

1. The coverage audit.
2. Onset estimation and causal ordering in the ranker.
3. The typed query algebra with world-recorded fixtures.
4. The hypothesis ledger with computed confidence.
5. The adversarial and metamorphic fixtures.

Everything else in feature 002 sits on those five.
